USING THIS MATTER / BOOK SITE: "THE SHORT PAGE"

Destination: https://the-companion-dossier.com/book

The page is a static part of the existing repository and Cloudflare Pages
site. It needs no framework, package install, server function, or new hosting
service, and the existing build.sh continues unchanged. Nothing outside book/
was modified.

THE CONCEPT
A short page for a stranger who has never heard of the book, the author, or
the project. It is modelled on a plain, fast book page (hook, trailer, who is
the missing man, one question to the visitor, six cards, the opening, the
offer) and it is a third shorter than the page before it: about 12,400 px
on a phone and 1,560 words.

Three rules shaped it.
  1. Plain words. Nothing on the page needs the book's vocabulary to be
     understood. The rite, "persona", "baseline", the glossary and the
     epigraph are in the novel, not here. The sentence a stranger reads first
     is "He built an AI that talks to the dead. Then he disappeared."
  2. One question, asked once. "Who would you bring back?" with a pair of
     empty brackets. A visitor may type a name, and then let it go. It is
     the one thing the page asks of the visitor, and it is the book's
     subject in one gesture: the voices we preserve, and the right to let go.
  3. Honest framing. The page says in the story section and again in the FAQ
     what is fiction and what is real. No review, rating, price, date or
     stock state is invented. The real machine, the talk and the paper are
     linked, never described as more than they are.

THE CONVERSION (book/config.js)
One setting decides what every primary button does.
  amazonUrl blank (now)  — primary buttons read "Read it free" and open the
                           free PDF; the offer leads with the free edition and
                           says the hardcover is coming soon.
  amazonUrl set          — every primary button (header, hero, cards, author,
                           phone dock) becomes "Get the hardcover" and opens
                           Amazon; the offer leads with the hardcover card and
                           its button; the free PDF stays one tap away; the
                           availability lines and ticker update.
  notifyUrl (optional)   — before publication only: the hardcover card adds
                           "Tell me when it ships" (any https link: a
                           mailing-list signup or a preorder page). Until this
                           or amazonUrl is set, a visitor who wants the
                           hardcover has nothing to press.
To publish: paste the Amazon URL (https://www.amazon.… or https://amzn.to/…),
commit, deploy, then open /book on a phone and a desktop and follow the
hardcover buttons once.

PAGE STRUCTURE (book/index.html)
  Ticker      — one line: a novel, the hook, "The machine in it is real",
                "Read it free tonight" (or the hardcover, once published)
  Header      — title; The story · Inside · Read the opening · Get the book;
                the primary button
  Hero        — #top. "A novel by Jacob E. Thomas", the hook as the headline,
                the turnable 3D cover, ONE button, two facts ("Free · the
                whole novel · no sign-up", "Hardcover coming soon"), the
                premise in three sentences ending on "one door too many", and
                one line saying what the book is: a horror novel told through
                what he left behind. On a phone the order is headline, cover,
                button, facts, premise.
  Trailer     — #trailer. "What answered him?" The 90-second trailer, the
                only film on the page, with captions and a transcript.
  The story   — #story. "Who is Dr. Thorne?" Three short paragraphs and the
                trailer's three questions. Then, in a ruled box (#real): "Dr.
                Thorne is a character in a novel. His machine is real." with
                one link to the real thing.
  The question— #question. "Who would you bring back?" / "His machine asks
                for one thing: a name." A pair of ember brackets holding an
                optional text field. Empty: "You already know whose name it
                would be." Typed: "You did not have to think about it." and
                one button, "Let them go"; the name leaves the brackets letter
                by letter; then "You let them go." Under it, the trailer's
                last line: "A mystery about the voices we preserve, and the
                right to let go." The page never answers as anyone.
  Inside      — #inside. "What you'll find inside": six numbered cards, each
                one plain image from the premise (the house that kept
                running; one door too many; a talk with a dead man; 3:11
                a.m.; the house was expecting her; the right to leave). A
                grid on desktop, a sideways swipe on a phone. Then the
                button.
  The posts   — #feed. "The posts started in March." In-world posts from
                spring 2027 (evidence.js, unchanged). Three show on desktop
                and two on a phone before "More posts".
  The opening — #first-page. The first three paragraphs of the novel,
                verbatim, on paper. Beside it, in plain view, how the novel
                was written, restating The Hand and the Glass (pp. v–vi),
                with a link to the author's statement.
  The offer   — #own (and the old #access). "Choose your way in." Two cards:
                "Read it free" (the whole novel, tonight, a PDF in the
                browser, no sign-up) and "Hold it" (the hardcover: 304 pages
                in full color, 6 × 9). Three photographs of pages (the Plate
                VII photo is cropped to the plate so the facing text cannot
                be read).
  From the
  house       — #house. "See for yourself." Three pieces of the record for the
                visitor who wants more before deciding: the floor plan (#plan,
                with the three counts the book prints: 11, 12, 11), the
                Franklin test as a chat that types itself, and 3:11:47 as a
                run log. The opt-in room tone ("Turn on the hum of the house")
                lives here.
  Questions   — #faq. Five: what kind of book; free or hardcover; is any of
                this real; what happened to the name I typed; how AI was used.
                Then the author, the footer with credits, and "Twelve?"

THE QUESTION AND PRIVACY
Whatever a visitor types in the brackets stays in the open tab. It is not
sent, not stored, not put in the URL, and never included in an engagement
event (the events are blank_filled and blank_released, with no detail). It is
erased when they press "Let them go" or close the tab. The page never
generates a reply. The FAQ says all of this in plain words. Without
JavaScript the field is a pair of empty brackets and the line under it.

QUOTATIONS
Every passage the page presents as the book's words carries
data-verbatim="<printed page>" (or "cover" for the back-cover copy) in
index.html and is an exact substring of that page of the print PDF. The Franklin
chat and the 3:11:47 log are staged from pp. 29–30 and 88–90: Franklin's lines
and the log lines are the book's; her typed lines, the stage directions and
the alert banner are the site's staging and are labelled so; the three
interrupted sentences are blacked out with bars of equal length so their shape
gives nothing away.

THE POSTS
In-world only. No fictional reader reviews, ratings or testimonials, on the
site or in the campaign; social proof is the real talk, paper and repository.
The voice memo (assets/posts/memo-014.m4a; in the feed as memo-014.mp4, a
720x900 recorder-screen video built from the same audio) is machine speech placed in a
recorded-at-night scene. Speech: Piper neural TTS, running locally, voice
en_US-libritts_r-medium, speaker 298 of 904 (LibriTTS-R audiobook readers,
CC BY 4.0, openslr.org/141). Each phrase is synthesized separately with its own
pacing, made hushed (breath blended in, softer top end, a slight tremor) and
mixed with the garage hum, crickets that stop when the car passes, wind on the
mic, footsteps and a screen door, through an emulated phone mic and automatic
gain control. Intelligibility is measured, not assumed: every phrase is
re-recorded until three Whisper recognizers (base/small/medium) hear exactly the
scripted words, then the best of several full takes is chosen by scoring a
decode of the final AAC file. Rebuild: python book/campaign/audio/make_memo_014.py
--punch --takes 5, then node book/campaign/audio/apply_memo_014.js, then
--feed book/campaign/audio/feed-bg.jpg (feed video), --reel
book/campaign/audio/reel-bg.jpg (social reel) and --ambient (room tone).

SPOILER POLICY
Copy stays inside the premise: the disappearance, the commission, the house,
the count, the Franklin test, three sealed rooms and the existence of the
interruption (its sentences are blacked out on the site), the illustration
that is listed and never shown, and what is already on the back cover ("The
archive was expecting her."; "a program that has abolished the right to
leave"; a document written before her arrival that begins to describe her
work). Nothing reveals Condition IV, the freezer, the sealed record, who hired
Miranda, whose program it is, or the ending. See campaign/README.md §3 for the
rules of the fiction.

MEASUREMENT
The page dispatches local DOM CustomEvents named book:engagement, with
name, placement and incoming utm_* values. Names: free_read, amazon_click,
trailer_play, volume_turn, memo_play, session_replay, alert_shown,
alert_replay, sound_on, notify_click, proof, evidence_more, nav, page_zoom,
blank_filled, blank_released. blank_filled against page views says how many
visitors answered the question; blank_released says how many let the name go.
Nothing is transmitted and no cookies or storage are used. Connect analytics
separately if wanted.

ACCESSIBILITY AND MOTION
The 3D book is keyboard-turnable (arrows, Enter). The name field is a labelled
text input ("A name. Optional. Nothing typed here leaves this page."), "Let
them go" is a button, and the result is announced once. The six cards are an
ordered list that scrolls with the keyboard on a phone-width screen. The floor
plan has full alt text. Transcripts fall back to static text.
prefers-reduced-motion disables grain, ticker, rings, glitch, typing, reveals
and the letter-by-letter fade. With JavaScript off the whole page is readable.
Layout checked at 320, 390, 768, 1024 and 1440 px with no horizontal overflow,
including with a long name, a right-to-left name or an emoji in the brackets.
The videos keep preload="none" and their caption tracks.
