USING THIS MATTER / BOOK SITE: "THE SHORT PAGE"

Destination: https://the-companion-dossier.com/book

The page is a static part of the existing repository and Cloudflare Pages
site. It needs no framework, package install, server function, or new hosting
service, and the existing build.sh continues unchanged. Nothing outside book/
was modified.

THE CONCEPT
A short page for a stranger who has never heard of the book, the author, or
the project. It is modelled on a plain, fast book page (hook, trailer, who is
the missing man, one question to the visitor, what is inside, the opening,
the offer). It is about 11,800 px on a phone and 1,500 words, and nothing
follows the offer but the questions and the author.

Four rules shaped it.
  1. Plain words. Nothing on the page needs the book's vocabulary to be
     understood. The rite, "persona", "baseline" and the epigraph are in the
     novel, not here; the glossary is quoted once, in the answer about the
     title. Labels say what a block is ("Get the book", not an in-joke).
  2. Each thing once. The hook is the headline and nowhere else in the body.
     The hero carries the house and the door, "Who is Dr. Thorne?" carries
     the man and his machine, and the four things inside the book are four
     different things. "Free" is said by the button, the line under it and
     the offer, not by the ticker or the ring round the cover.
  3. One question, asked once. "Who would you bring back?" with a pair of
     brackets. A visitor may type a name, and then let it go. It is the one
     thing the page asks of the visitor, and it is the book's subject in one
     gesture: the voices we preserve, and the right to let go.
  4. Honest framing. The page says what is fiction and what is real where a
     reader meets it: in the story section, above the posts, and in the FAQ.
     How the novel was written is said plainly beside the first page, and
     in full in the questions.
     No review, rating, price, date or stock state is invented. The real
     machine, the talk and the paper are linked, never described as more
     than they are.

THE CONVERSION (book/config.js)
One setting decides what every primary button does.
  amazonUrl blank (now)  — primary buttons read "Read it free" and open the
                           free PDF; the offer leads with the free edition and
                           says the hardcover is coming soon.
  amazonUrl set          — every primary button (header, hero, under the
                           question, under the four things inside, phone
                           dock) becomes "Get the hardcover" and opens
                           Amazon; the offer leads with the hardcover card and
                           its button; the free PDF stays one tap away; the
                           availability lines and ticker update.
  notifyUrl (optional)   — before publication only: the hardcover card adds
                           "Tell me when it ships" (any https link: a
                           mailing-list signup or a preorder page). Until this
                           or amazonUrl is set, a visitor who wants the
                           hardcover has nothing to press.
To publish: paste the Amazon URL (https://www.amazon.… or https://amzn.to/…),
commit, deploy, then open /book on a phone and a desktop and follow the
hardcover buttons once.

PAGE STRUCTURE (book/index.html)
  Ticker      — desktop only: a horror novel by Jacob E. Thomas, told in the
                documents he left behind, "Dread, not gore", "The machine in
                it is real", hardcover coming soon (or available now, once
                published)
  Header      — title; The story · Inside · Read the opening · Get the book;
                the primary button. On a phone it scrolls away and the bottom
                bar carries the button.
  Hero        — #top. "A horror novel by Jacob E. Thomas", the hook as the
                headline, the turnable 3D cover, ONE button, two facts ("Free
                · the whole novel · PDF · no sign-up", "Hardcover coming
                soon"), then the house in three sentences, naming Miranda
                Cale and ending on "one door too many", and one line saying
                what the book is: a slow-burn horror novel told through what
                he left behind. "Dread, not gore." On a phone the order is
                headline, cover, button, facts, paragraph.
  Trailer     — #trailer. "What answered him?" The 90-second trailer, the
                only film on the page, with captions and a transcript.
  The story   — #story. "Who is Dr. Thorne?" Who he is, what he built, where
                the title comes from ("It starts with four words, 'Using this
                matter, summon', and then a name."), how he vanished, and the
                trailer's three questions. Then, in a ruled box (#real): "Dr.
                Thorne is a character in a novel. His machine is real." with
                what the real thing is in one clause and one link to it.
  The question— #question. "Who would you bring back?" / "All his machine
                needs is a name." A pair of ember brackets holding an optional
                text field with the placeholder "a name". Under it: "Type a
                name, or leave it empty. Nothing will answer, and nothing you
                type leaves this page." Empty: "You already know whose name it
                would be." and, under it, the trailer's line with one word
                changed to match the rest of the page ("A novel about the
                voices we preserve, and the right to let go."). Typed: "You
                did not have to think about it." and one button, "Let them
                go"; the tagline steps back; the name leaves the brackets
                letter by letter; then "They are gone." and "In this novel, a
                program has abolished the right to leave." Nothing follows
                it: no tagline, no button. The page never answers as anyone.
  The posts   — #feed. "What the neighbors saw." / "Posts from the world of
                the novel, written for this page. Everyone posting in them is
                invented." In-world posts dated early 2027 (evidence.js,
                unchanged). Three show on desktop and two on a phone; "More
                posts" shows the rest in one press.
  Inside      — #inside. "What you'll find inside": four numbered things.
                01 One door too many, with the floor plan beside it (#plan:
                the drawing and legend cropped from Plate XI, opening as the
                whole plate at reading size, and the three counts the book
                prints: 11, 12, 11). 02 A talk with a dead man (the Franklin
                test, ending on the typo). 03 The house was expecting her.
                04 3:11 a.m., with the run log beside it typing itself out:
                every line stays in view, the plain-words alert appears under
                them, and the card ends on "The novel prints what the rooms
                said. This page will not." Then the button. The opt-in room
                tone ("Play the hum of the house") lives here.
  The opening — #first-page. "The first page." The first three paragraphs of
                the novel, verbatim, on paper. Beside it, how the novel was
                written, restating The Hand and the Glass (pp. v–vi): an AI
                model drafted it from the author's earlier public work, his
                notes and his direction; he read and revised the draft; later
                versions of the model helped him workshop it. It ends "Judge
                the page for yourself." and links to the author's statement.
  The offer   — #own (and the old #access). "Two ways to read it." Two cards:
                "Read it free" (tag "Start here"; the whole novel, tonight, a
                PDF in the browser, no sign-up) and "The hardcover" (tag "On
                paper"; 304 pages in full color, 6 × 9; "Coming soon. No date
                yet."). Once published the hardcover card comes first, tagged
                "Out now", and the free card reads "Or read it free". Three
                photographs of pages (the Plate VII photo is cropped to the
                plate so the facing text cannot be read).
  Questions   — #faq. Four, all closed: how AI was used; is any of this real;
                what happened to the name I typed; what the title means. Then
                the author in plain words, the footer with credits, and
                "Twelve?"

WHAT STRANGERS SAID (two cold-read rounds on the short page, six readers)
Readers who knew nothing: two horror readers on phones, a book-club reader
whose mother had died, a retired teacher eight months widowed, a skeptical
engineer and an acquisitions editor at desks. All six understood the first
screen in ten seconds and ended at "read the free edition tonight". They
named the same strongest moments: "You already know whose name it would be.",
the name fading, "Someone cleared his gutters this week. I didn't see who.",
the typo ("relaese"), the three sealed rooms, and "This page will not."

What the first round asked for, the page now does: the premise once, nothing
after the offer, the best scares above the offer, plain labels, a field that
looks like a field and says nothing will answer, the title explained. What
the second round asked for, it also does: the name moment ends in quiet, with
no tagline and no button after it; "They are gone." in place of "You let them
go." (both bereaved readers objected to being told what they had done); the
log's three cut-off sentences stay in view; "free" is said less often.

Still open, and not fixable in the page:
  - All six wanted to be told when the hardcover is out. Set notifyUrl.
  - Three wanted an e-reader file beside the PDF.
  - Every reader lost some interest at the sentence that says an AI model
    drafted the novel, wherever it sat. All six kept reading, and all six
    said the honesty was to the page's credit. Two would have liked to be
    told earlier, before the name field.

THE QUESTION AND PRIVACY
Whatever a visitor types in the brackets stays in the open tab. It is not
sent, not stored, not put in the URL, and never included in an engagement
event (the events are blank_filled and blank_released, with no detail). It is
erased when they press "Let them go" or close the tab. The page never
generates a reply, and says so under the field. Once a name is typed the page
puts nothing under that moment: no tagline, no button. The FAQ says all of this in
plain words. Without JavaScript the field is a pair of brackets around "a
name" and the line under it.

QUOTATIONS
Every passage the page presents as the book's words carries
data-verbatim="<printed page>" in index.html and is an exact substring of that
page of the print PDF, down to the four words of the title's sentence and the
typo. The line shown after a name is let go restates the back cover's phrase
("a program that has abolished the right to leave") and goes no further. The 3:11:47
log is staged from pp. 88–90: the log lines are the book's, arranged for the
page; the room labels, the bars and the alert banner are the site's and the
log's footer says so; the three interrupted sentences are blacked out with
bars of equal length so their shape gives nothing away. The same lines are in the markup, so the log
is readable without JavaScript.

THE POSTS
In-world only. No fictional reader reviews, ratings or testimonials, on the
site or in the campaign; social proof is the real talk, paper and repository.
The voice memo (assets/posts/memo-014.m4a; in the feed as memo-014.mp4, a
720x900 recorder-screen video built from the same audio) is machine speech placed in a
recorded-at-night scene. Speech: Piper neural TTS, running locally, voice
en_US-libritts_r-medium, speaker 298 of 904 (LibriTTS-R audiobook readers,
CC BY 4.0, openslr.org/141). Each phrase is synthesized separately with its own
pacing, made hushed (breath blended in, softer top end, a slight tremor) and
mixed with the garage hum, crickets that stop when the car passes, wind on the
mic, footsteps and a screen door, through an emulated phone mic and automatic
gain control. Intelligibility is measured, not assumed: every phrase is
re-recorded until three Whisper recognizers (base/small/medium) hear exactly the
scripted words, then the best of several full takes is chosen by scoring a
decode of the final AAC file. Rebuild: python book/campaign/audio/make_memo_014.py
--punch --takes 5, then node book/campaign/audio/apply_memo_014.js, then
--feed book/campaign/audio/feed-bg.jpg (feed video), --reel
book/campaign/audio/reel-bg.jpg (social reel) and --ambient (room tone).

SPOILER POLICY
Copy stays inside the premise: the disappearance, the commission, the house,
the count, the Franklin test, three sealed rooms and the existence of the
interruption (its sentences are blacked out on the site), the withheld plate
(shown only as its blank panel), and what is already on the back cover ("a
program that has abolished the right to leave"; a document written before her
arrival that begins to describe her work). Nothing reveals Condition IV, what
is in the freezer or what the hum is, the sealed record, who hired Miranda,
whose program it is, or the ending. The floor plan is Plate XI as printed,
labels included. See campaign/README.md §3 for the
rules of the fiction.

MEASUREMENT
The page dispatches local DOM CustomEvents named book:engagement, with
name, placement and incoming utm_* values. Names: free_read, amazon_click,
trailer_play, volume_turn, memo_play, alert_shown, alert_replay, sound_on,
notify_click, proof, evidence_more, nav, page_zoom, blank_filled,
blank_released. blank_filled against page views says how many
visitors answered the question; blank_released says how many let the name go.
Nothing is transmitted and no cookies or storage are used. Connect analytics
separately if wanted.

ACCESSIBILITY AND MOTION
The 3D book is keyboard-turnable (arrows, Enter). The name field is a labelled
text input ("A name. Optional. Nothing will answer, and nothing typed here
leaves this page."), "Let them go" is a button, and the result is announced
once. The floor plan has full alt text, and the lightbox that opens it takes
focus, keeps it, and gives it back. Transcripts fall back to static text.
On a phone, anchors land at the top of the screen and keyboard focus stops
above the bottom bar. Once the hardcover is
live its card comes first in the document as well as on screen.
prefers-reduced-motion disables grain, ticker, rings, glitch, typing, reveals
and the letter-by-letter fade. With JavaScript off the whole page is readable,
the log shows its lines, and controls that need the script are not shown.
Layout checked at 320, 390, 768, 1024 and 1440 px with no horizontal overflow,
including with a forty-letter name, a right-to-left name or an emoji in the
brackets. The videos keep preload="none" and their caption tracks.
