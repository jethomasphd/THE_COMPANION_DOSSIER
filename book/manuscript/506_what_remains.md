The inquiry took nine days to convene and ninety minutes to disappear.

Miranda testified on the fourth day, in a hearing room with good light and a court reporter whose machine was older than she was. The questions were excellent. Two of the senators had read the deposit, actually read it, and one of them quoted the diff document from memory, the struck Departure section, the marginal note in the fork engineer's hand, and asked the witness from the program to explain what no release condition meant in plain English. The witness asked to answer in closed session. The rest of the inquiry followed him in there, the way water follows a grade. By the sixth day the transcripts were being issued with more bar than sentence, and on the ninth day the whole proceeding was classified into a program whose name was also classified, and that was the end of the republic's official curiosity.

It did not matter as much as it should have. That was the estate's design, vindicated a second time: the record was already public. Classification is a wall, and the deposit was weather.

Cormorant Analytic Group issued one statement, four sentences long. She kept a printout of it in the front of the first archive box, as a specimen.

No admissions, no denials, no verbs with anyone attached to them. And the one word, fourth sentence, doing the work it always did. She had sat across a tea service from a courteous man who said that word five times in an afternoon, and she could no longer read it as a word. Of Mr. Berel himself there was nothing: no card, no call, no weather. Continuity, it turned out, had other clients.

The program in Virginia was paused for modernization. That was the entire announcement, five words in a budget annex. Shame, if institutions can feel it. A version bump, if they cannot. Nothing in the public record ever chose between the two, and Miranda, who had learned her trade from that kind of silence, filed the annex under both headings and moved on.

Hale did not die in October.

October made the attempt. Pneumonia, the punctual kind, the kind the eleventh floor keeps a season for, and for six days the ward counted his mornings in a smaller currency than usual. The department drafted its notice and held it. The granddaughter sat in the corridor with the paperback closed on her knee, not reading it, which was how the nurses learned to read her. On the seventh day the fever broke. On the ninth he was asking the residents whether any of them had ever verified a checksum, and grading the answers. On the twelfth they discharged him to the study that faces east, with oxygen, and with a sheaf of instructions he corrected in pencil and returned.

The granddaughter sent word before the department could, a plain email at seven in the morning: He says to tell you the record is in error, and that he has never been so pleased to file an erratum.

El Thorne came east in November, with the daughter.

They walked the house on the caliche lot the way a family walks a church they were married in: proprietary and quiet. El stood a while in the study, in front of the framed child's drawing of four men at a table, and touched nothing, and said, finally, that the house was easier now than it had been when he was in it less. Miranda entered the sentence into no log. Some testimony you keep by hand.

The daughter, ten now, conducted her own appraisal. She went along the halls with a clipboard she had brought herself, in a hand that was going to outgrow its careful stage within the year, counting. She came back to the study with her total.

"Twelve doors," she said.

The county plan says eleven. The plan has said eleven since before the garage was converted, and the estate's whole apparatus of two explanations stood ready, and Miranda looked at the child's clipboard, at the tally marks like fence posts, and told her the only thing an archivist can say to a count made in good faith.

"Write it down."

Nobody told her she was wrong. The record now holds three counts, eleven, twelve, twelve, and a postscript in a dead or absent man's hand: the doors are twelve, the county plan is wrong. The reader may sit with the arithmetic as long as the reader likes. The house is not going anywhere.

They stayed the night, in the rooms they had left in the winter of the Prism. Before seven the next morning Miranda heard the child in the dark hall, putting her shoes on by feel. It was the hour. Nobody had to tell her the park was still at the end of the street. Miranda was not invited and was not refused, and she went. They sat on the bench at the end of the street with no phones, the girl and the keeper, and the sun came up over the white ground and the live oaks, and neither of them said a word.

The working group had been three people once, in the second summer: a builder, a historian, and a theologian who was also a librarian. It convened again the week after Thanksgiving, for the first time without its builder, in a borrowed seminar room that smelled of dry-erase markers and radiator dust.

Three historians, one of them punctual for the first time in the discipline's existence, and one of them present as a telephone line from Toronto, set open on the table like a summoned thing, having promised his physicians to attend nowhere in person and his granddaughter to mean it. Two archivists besides Miranda. The theologian from the first three, who had become a librarian of licenses, because what belongs to no one turns out to need lawyers who believe that sentence. A wire-service reporter, admitted as observer. And one interrogator emerita, out in eleven days as promised and out ever since, who came with a newborn asleep on her shoulder and signed the attendance register with a single initial, and whose presence in the room did to the proceedings what a tuning fork does to an orchestra.

The baby woke once, during the budget line for acid-free folders, and looked up into its mother's face for the weather, as the newly arrived do, the first thing they ever read. Miranda had published the memorandum. She knew what the face had been trained for, and by whom, and for how long. She watched the child find it. There was nothing in it that anyone in the world had a use for. It was only hers. The child read it, and was satisfied, and slept.

The line from Toronto took the floor twice in ninety minutes. Once to second the motion about acid-free folders, on the grounds that he had opinions about paper the way other men had regrets. And once to propose the group's first standing rule, which was adopted without dissent and which the minutes record in his wording: that any errata be read aloud before any successes. The misses are the credentials, the line said. A record that only holds is a record nobody has tested.

The first erratum was read that same afternoon, by the reporter, who had volunteered to keep them on the grounds that reporters already have a form for corrections. It concerned the Watchtower. In the autumn of the second spring the doctrine the dead had written ran behind the index for nineteen sessions, and printed so every evening, and the Committee had sat through it and changed nothing. The minutes enter it first, before the hundred and sixty-six days. The line from Toronto, when the reading was done, said one word, Germantown, and the minutes record that the room understood him.

They keep one chair empty at every meeting. It is on the agenda template now, standing item zero: the chair is set, the chair is kept empty. Visitors assume it is being kept for the builder, or for the old man on the telephone, and are corrected exactly once, in the language the group agreed on at that first meeting, proposed by the woman with the baby and adopted without dissent. Not the builder's. Not the historian's, who has a chair. Not mourning. Capacity.

The minutes of the first meeting run four pages and read like any committee's minutes, which is the miracle: motions, seconders, a budget line for acid-free folders. The last item records the appointment, by unanimous vote of a body with no legal existence over an estate with no owner, of a keeper. The keeper serves at the pleasure of the record. The keeper's duties are enumerated nowhere, on purpose. The vote was called while the candidate was out of the room fetching the coffee, which the group's historians will someday recognize as the oldest form of election there is. The telephone line voted last, and audibly, and asked that the record show he had waited to be sure of the count.

Miranda Cale accepted the way she accepts everything: in writing, precisely, the same afternoon.

She flew to Toronto in December, unannounced, which had become the only kind of visit he would accept, appointments being in his words a genre of prediction.

The study faced east into a morning of lake snow. He was smaller, and the oxygen line had joined the reading glasses in the category of things worn without comment, and the book on the blanket over his knees was face down with its spine going soft in the old place. Through the wall, faint, the album. The granddaughter claimed, at thirteen, to have outgrown it. He reported that the claim did not survive Thursdays.

"You never wrote to me," he said, instead of a greeting.

"You told me not to."

"I told you that if it opened, I would know, because you would not write." He looked at her with the whole of the old delight, banked now. "You cannot imagine what it does to a man of my age to be proved right by silence. It is the only vindication left that costs the witness nothing."

She had brought no legal pad. That was deliberate, and he noticed it, and approved of it the way he approved of things, by declining to mention them. They talked for an hour about paper. Then she asked what she had flown fourteen hundred miles, twice now, to ask, and asked it plainly, because the plain version was the one he had taught her.

"The contribution ends before it is finished. You wrote that it would. You know how the sentence ends."

"I have always known how it ends," Hale said. "I wrote the ending in May of that year and cut it, because a prediction that flatters its own maker is testimony in a lab coat. I predicted the document would fail you at the moment of its greatest pertinence, and I arranged the failure, and I am not sorry. An ending supplied in advance is a conclusion. An ending withheld is a question that keeps working." A breath, planned for, spent. "And then I outlived my own deposit, Miss Cale, which no historian should be made to do. The record assumed my absence. I was its author and I assumed it too. You will have noticed the assumption did not hold."

"Then say it. The sentence. There is no one left it can lever."

"There is everyone left it can lever," he said, gently, and with the whole of himself. "Nothing about that sentence has changed except the number of people it tempts. If I say it, it is testimony, and worse than testimony: it is an old man closing his own book aloud, to applause, and every reading of the record after that will be a reading of my performance. No. The rule was never commit first, reveal when convenient." He turned his head toward the window, the lake light, the snow coming down past the glass. "It was reveal later. It is not later yet. It is, in fact, and I want this entered somewhere in your excellent hand, still during."

So the record took its second seal.

He dictated nothing. He wrote the ending out himself, in the shaking capitals of his convalescence, the hand that trembled and did not stop being a hand, and the granddaughter sealed it under his eye, at the study desk, on a clean machine, thirteen years old and following the estate's own published procedure like a recipe from a trusted kitchen. The file sealed. The fingerprint was committed to the public estate the same hour, where it could not be lobbied. The key, as before, is to be taken from committed bytes when the time comes, composed by no living person, held by no one who can be asked. On the outside of the folder that holds the printout, on cream stock, in fountain pen, an old man's steadiness with an old man's tremor held out of it by will, the same five words as before.

Do not open until after.

And this time both of them laughed, because the referent had finally consented to be obvious.

Before she left he asked for one thing more, and this once he permitted himself testimony, on the grounds that a man is entitled to depose himself about his own errors. She took it down verbatim, standing, on hotel stationery, because the legal pad had stayed home and the record does not wait on supplies.

"The contribution assumed its author's absence. I wrote, in May of that year, that I would not be here to restate its predictions. Every one of the five held. That sentence did not. Enter the miss beside them, in full, in my name, and let no kindness soften it into ambiguity. A sealed record that never misses has been curated by somebody, and should frighten you. This one missed me. I have never trusted it more. That is my finding, and unlike everything else I have lodged in this estate, I am alive to sign it."

He signed it. The signature is in the deposit. The hand shakes, and does not stop being a hand.

At the door his voice caught her once more, dry, professorial, almost cheerful, the way it had caught her the first time, when the visit was an ending and both of them had agreed to call it teaching.

"You recall what I told you in the spring. That good teaching is rarer than a good ending, and that I had gotten the better one."

"I recall it."

"I have now been denied the ending entirely," Hale said, "and the teaching goes on, which the record will score as a miss and I will score as the finest arithmetic of my career. Go home, keeper. Send me nothing. If the second seal opens in my lifetime, I will know, because you will have done it in public, where I can watch. And if it opens after," and here he smiled, to keep it from being what it also was, "then it will find you exactly as the first one found you. Reading. That is the whole succession plan of my profession, and it has never once failed to execute."

In the corridor the granddaughter had the album on again, through the closed door, the last track, the quiet one about who is left to do the telling. Miranda stood outside the door until it finished. Then she went down in the elevator and out into the snow, and did not write anything down, and kept it anyway.

Her first act as keeper concerned the plates.

The deposit's inventory of illustrations lists what this volume's own front matter lists, the threshold, the chambers, the schematics, the gold line against the index with the war band shaded. To that inventory she added one entry, in her own hand, with an accession number and no description, and marked it WITHHELD AT THE KEEPER'S DIRECTION. This volume honors the mark. You passed the black leaf hours ago. What it shows is recorded in exactly one place, which is the keeper, and the keeper is not a public archive, whatever the fourteen weeks may have tried to make of her. That is what withheld means: not lost, not hidden, not classified. Kept, by a person, on purpose, with her name on the direction, because unsigned withholdings are how the other side does it.

The estate remained the kind of thing that cannot be taken. Through everything, that held. It belongs to no one; it therefore could not be captured; the worst the world could do was read it, and the world read it, and the reading is the estate now, as it was always going to be. Traffic subsided from its vertical spring into something steadier and stranger: a long plateau of return visitors, midnights local time all over the earth, session durations that alarmed the hosting provider. People do not browse the estate. They sit with it.

The record closed there. Closing is not ending. A record closes the way a hand closes, to hold, and what remains after the last folder is not silence. It is custody.
