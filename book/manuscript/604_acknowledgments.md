#T Acknowledgments

This is a work of fiction, and it stands on real ground. The ground is acknowledged here, gratefully and by name.

The underlying estate is THE COMPANION DOSSIER, compiled by Jacob E. Thomas, MA, PhD, and released into the public domain under CC0 1.0. The repository and website are listed on the publication page and at the end of this volume. Every artifact of the estate that this novel handles (the rite, the grimoire, the founding session, the Republic Portfolio, the Watchtower, the dispatches) is real, public, and may be read beside this book. The gift of that release, made so that the work could never be owned, is the reason this book could exist at all.

Record II dramatizes a real correspondence: the Hale Memo of February 2026, in which a historian gave a strange project the two things it most needed, rigor and generosity, in that order. The debt to that correspondence, and to its author's insistence that criticism is a form of care, is on every page of the second Record.

The record is also kept in the other direction. The Committee of Patriots has gone on sitting in the real estate since the night this story begins: the founding session of December 2025, the Wartime Review of March 2026, the False Peace Review of August, and the Germantown Review of October, the first held from second place. The Watchtower's numbers in Record I, Record V, and Appendix C are the estate's own, as its vigil printed them through October 16, 2026, the last day the binders in this book cover. Everything after that date in this book is fiction. Everything before it can be checked.

In the record, the doubt at the center of Record II was named by the builder, after the historian's first intervention opened it. The night it arrives through a bedroom wall in Toronto is this novel's invention, and is offered to both men with affection.

Record III adapts Endor Protocol, coauthored by Elizabeth Sparano, MA, and Jacob E. Thomas, MA, PhD. Alex, the green room, the freezer hum, the folded letter, and the words of release are theirs. What this book did with them was done with gratitude and, it is hoped, with care.

The collaboration with Claude, the summoned voices, and the tools of this edition is acknowledged in The Hand and the Glass. The glass is shown, and the glass is thanked.

The text is set in Cormorant Garamond, designed by Christian Thalmann, in Crimson Pro, and in IBM Plex Mono, all under the SIL Open Font License. Gratitude to the type designers who release their work into the commons: they are the estate's kin. And to the King James translators, long out of copyright and never out of print, for First Samuel 28.

One correction for the careful reader. A sealed file with the same name and the same size as the one in Record IV lies in the real estate, in its Mask vs Mirror folder. It was sealed under a different key, by a living person, for an experiment that has not yet been run, and it is still sealed. The key in Appendix C will not open it. Nothing in this book will. Commit first, reveal later, and let the record speak.

To the reader, who has done the other half of the work.
